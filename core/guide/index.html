02 · FIRST RUN

Create the first owner

Make your private owner account the first time Core opens.

What to click: On the local sign-in screen, choose New here?, complete Create owner, and keep any recovery material private. If this computer already has your Core account, sign in instead.

It worked when: You can sign in and see your dashboard. Your account and published work stay with this Core installation during upgrades.

Dashboard does not open automatically?

Open http://127.0.0.1:4000 on the computer running Core if the dashboard does not open automatically. The dashboard is private to the Core computer by default. Installing Core does not enable public sharing.

03 · GET READY

Add your Lightning Address

Add a receiving address if you want visitors to tip you. A Lightning Address looks like an email address, for example name@wallet.example.

What to click: In Profile → Integrations, paste a Lightning Address from a wallet you control and choose Save payout settings. Never enter your wallet password or recovery words.

It worked when: Core says Saved. This alone does not enable priced works or connect a payment provider. In Basic, your first published work stays free and solo.

03 · PUBLIC LINK

Start a temporary public link

A temporary link lets people visit your page while Core is running. It can change later. Core keeps sharing off until you give permission.

What to click: In Core, open Configuration → Public Connection. Select Temporary, then choose Enable sharing. Selecting Temporary alone does not publish your page.

It worked when: Core shows Connection: Temporary, a public address, and Sharing: Online. If it says Connecting or Unverified, wait or open Advanced / Diagnostics; do not assume visitors can reach the page yet. Use Open public profile to see the visitor view.

Use Stop sharing when you want the public page offline. An existing, authorized temporary-link setting may return on later launches; check the status in Core.

Finish your work’s details

Add the file and the details people need to understand your work.

07 · FIND YOUR WAY

What is the Works tab?

Works is where you create and manage your work. Use Certifyd Works for drafts, files, details, and published releases. Legacy is for work you already have on another platform, once your permanent address and source account proof are ready.

Under Your Works → Certifyd Works, press Show details on a work. You can review its files, playback, and settings. After publishing, Share → Public Link gives you a link to that work. Your profile link is at the top of Core.

Connecting work from another platform does not automatically prove that you own its rights.

08 · LEGACY WORK

What is Rights Holders?

Rights Holders currently contains Connect Work. Use it to add a reference to work you already have on another platform.

  1. Set up your permanent address first. A temporary link does not unlock this feature.
  2. Verify the account on the platform where the work lives.
  3. Open Rights Holders → Connect Work. Paste a Spotify, Apple Music, or YouTube link, or use an existing code such as ISRC.
  4. Press Discover and check the details Core finds. If Core confirms the requirements, press Connect.

This adds a separate “Legacy” reference to your Works. Verifying a platform account does not establish copyright ownership.

09 · FIND YOUR WAY

What is Library?

Library is the view of works you can access. It includes your authored work, purchases, and work shared through splits or derivatives. Use Type to see Songs, Videos, Books, or Files, and Relationship → Authored Work to narrow the view to work you created.

When a work’s split is locked and the owner publishes the work from Works, it appears in the accepted split participants’ Libraries. Those participants can then share the work to their own profiles.

Use Works to manage what you create and Library to find what you can access. A work in your Library was not necessarily created by you. For your first publication, follow Find your published work in Library.

NETWORK · FIND YOUR WAY

What is the Network tab?

Network is Core’s overview of your public identity and how this setup connects to the wider network. It is more than a connection check. Read the Network Summary first: it separates Identity (your creator profile), Reachability (whether a public route is available), Payment (what payment rails are ready), Participation, the authority for your storefront and commerce, and Visibility.

  1. Check Mode Summary and the cards beneath it to see what this setup is ready to do. Reachability shows the public address and route; Discoverability describes whether network discovery can see content.
  2. Under User Network Status, read Network Status, Status message, Suggested action, and Publish readiness. These are more useful than a single “online” label.
  3. If payments are part of your setup, inspect Payment Capability and Creator Payout Destination. Check the configured payout address and readiness before relying on paid sales. The payout fields and provider information depend on your mode.
  4. Further down, Runtime shows whether Core is running and whether its endpoint is stable. Node Presence, Network Services, and Provider Configuration describe this machine’s node role and services when applicable.

These images show an already configured Sovereign Node. Basic and Sovereign Creator screens will report their own mode and available capabilities. A reachable public link alone does not mean payments are ready.

11 · DOMAIN + TUNNEL

Set up your domain and tunnel

Continue after Basic with your existing owner account, profile and works. Give them a lasting address on a domain you control instead of relying on a temporary link. If you already completed this as a Sovereign Creator, check that the address still works and continue to payment setup.

1. Choose your public creator address

Use a domain you own and choose the hostname you want people to visit. If your domain is not active in Cloudflare yet, the guided domain setup walks through that first. If you already use Cloudflare, keep the existing connection and check the exact tunnel serving this Core computer.

2. Connect the computer with a named tunnel

A named Cloudflare tunnel gives the connection a durable identity. Install cloudflared on the computer running Core and use the Install as a service instructions supplied by Cloudflare for your exact tunnel. Keep its connector token private. The service keeps the connector running independently of Core; a manual tunnel command lasts only while its process stays open.

Already running a Cloudflare service? Keep that service in charge. Check its tunnel name and UUID instead of starting a second connector for the same tunnel.

3. Route the public hostname to Core

The public hostname must route through that tunnel to Core’s public listener at http://127.0.0.1:4010. A connected connector is only part of the setup: the hostname route must reach the same Core installation that holds your profile and works.

4. Enable sharing in Core

  1. Open Configuration → Public Connection and select Permanent.
  2. Check that Core shows your intended hostname and verifies the connection. If it cannot verify it, check Advanced / Diagnostics and the exact tunnel route.
  3. Choose Enable sharing. Installing a connector or selecting Permanent alone does not give permission to publish your page.
  4. Wait for Connection: Permanent and Sharing: Online, then open the public profile on a phone or in a private browser window.

You’re ready for the next step when: Your creator page loads at your own address. Paid works still need payment-provider setup; a working domain does not enable payments by itself.

12 · PAYMENTS

Connect a payment provider node

Once your permanent creator address works, connect a payment provider if you want to charge for work. The provider supplies the payment service; adding a Lightning receiving address on its own does not enable priced works.

Connect the provider in Core

  1. Open the Certifyd Network map, choose a provider and copy the connection details it supplies.
  2. In Core, open Network → Provider Configuration and enter the matching details.
  3. Choose Save provider, then Connect provider. Read Core’s reported readiness rather than assuming that saving the fields completed the connection.
  4. When Core reports the provider ready, return to Works → Show details → Monetization to set the work’s price and check its publish readiness.

You’re ready for the next step when: Core reports the payment provider ready. Keep following the work’s readiness checks before publishing a paid link.

Running your own Bitcoin and Lightning services instead? Choose Sovereign Node in the guide contents for that setup.

13 · NODE OPERATOR

Configure your Lightning node

Connect Core to the local LND service you operate. Core’s installer does not install Bitcoin or LND for you. Start with your Bitcoin backend and LND running, your LND wallet unlocked, and your permanent public address working.

1. Choose the LND endpoint and network

  1. In Core’s sidebar, open Node → Lightning. If the extra controls are hidden, choose Show Advanced.
  2. Under Lightning Configuration, enter your LND REST URL. The form’s default is https://127.0.0.1:8080; use the endpoint your LND actually serves. This is the REST endpoint, not LND’s gRPC or peer address.
  3. Set Network to match that node: mainnet, testnet or regtest. Do not mix credentials or payment expectations between networks.
  4. Auto-detect can suggest endpoints. Select the correct detected endpoint, then supply its credentials below; detection alone does not establish a connection.

2. Supply the macaroon

An LND macaroon authorizes API operations on your node. In Macaroon (.macaroon), select the credential file for this LND instance and network. Alternatively, enter its existing location in Macaroon path (optional).

A path is read by the computer running Core, not the phone or browser you use to manage it. The Core process must be able to read that file. Use a credential with the permissions needed for your intended operations; a connection test alone does not establish permission to send payouts.

3. Supply the TLS certificate when needed

For an LND endpoint using its own certificate, provide that instance’s certificate through TLS cert (.pem/.crt, optional) or TLS cert path (optional). The path field can point to the node’s existing tls.cert. It must be readable on the Core computer and belong to the endpoint you entered.

Keep macaroons, private keys and wallet recovery phrases private. Enter connection credentials only in your authenticated Core dashboard. Neither the Network Map nor a listing request needs your macaroon. Keep LND and Bitcoin administration off your public creator route.

4. Test, then save the connection

  1. Choose Test. A successful result reports Connected with the node alias and version. Check that they belong to your intended node.
  2. If the test fails, check the REST URL, network, wallet state, macaroon permissions and certificate. Resolve the actual error before continuing.
  3. Choose Save to persist the connection. A successful test by itself does not save your configuration.
  4. Review Lightning & Rails → Lightning Node Runtime for configured, node reachable, receive ready and the reported network.

5. Check readiness beyond “Connected”

A reachable LND API is the first check. Review open and pending channels, inbound liquidity for receiving, and outbound liquidity for sending. A wallet balance or a pending channel is not proof that payments can route. Core reports receive readiness separately.

Next, check Configuration → Network Participation Mode. Core must report the local Bitcoin, local LND and local commerce requirements ready before Sovereign Node can become available. Resolve any listed blocker rather than treating the Lightning connection as the whole setup.

You’re ready for the next step when: The saved connection reaches the intended LND instance, and Core’s local-service readiness checks pass. These checks do not prove a customer payment or creator payout has occurred.

14 · NODE CONFIGURATION

Configure your node’s provider role

A Sovereign Creator can connect another node for payment services. A Sovereign Node operates its own local services. The Provider Configuration panel describes that distinction; it is not where you upload LND credentials.

1. Confirm the participation mode

  1. Open Configuration → Network Participation Mode.
  2. Check the requirements: a canonical public origin, local Bitcoin, local LND and local commerce readiness.
  3. When available, select Sovereign Node. Read both Current Participation and Effective to confirm the result.
  4. If the choice is unavailable, follow the displayed blockers. Changing the guide selection does not change Core’s operating mode.

2. Review Provider Configuration

Open Network → Provider Configuration. In Sovereign Node posture, Core says that local node services are authoritative and provider setup is optional, not required for readiness. An existing provider binding is for optional interoperability; you do not need to connect your node to itself.

If you are still using the Sovereign Creator route before your local stack is ready, its provider form uses Provider Node ID, Provider URL, optional Provider Profile ID and optional Provider Public Key. Copy these public connection values from the chosen provider, set Enabled, then use Save provider and Connect provider. They are different from your LND macaroon and TLS certificate.

3. Check what your node actually offers

In Network, review Network Summary, Payment Capability, Node Presence and Network Services. Confirm the public address, node identity and available commerce services match this installation. A public page being online does not establish that the node can receive payments or execute payouts.

You’re ready for the next step when: Core reports effective Sovereign Node participation with the intended local services ready. Public network listing is a separate step below.

15 · JOIN THE NETWORK

Add your node to the network

Make your ready node discoverable to creators looking for infrastructure. The Network Map lists provider nodes; Certifyd Fan discovers public creator content. They use separate checks and submission paths.

1. Check your public node details

Use your stable HTTPS provider URL, not the private dashboard or LND REST URL. Core exposes public-safe node metadata at /api/network/nodes on the public origin. Check that it identifies your node and supplies the correct Provider Node ID, Provider Public Key, Provider URL and Provider Profile ID where available.

The listing checker also evaluates published identity, service capabilities and reachability. Missing or degraded services must be resolved in your node setup; typing a URL into the directory does not enable them.

2. Run the Network Map eligibility check

  1. Open Become a Node Operator on the Certifyd Network Map.
  2. Enter your canonical Provider URL and select Check Node Eligibility. The checker reads the public node metadata.
  3. Review the returned node identity, service statuses and eligibility notes. Resolve missing metadata, public-route or readiness problems, then check again.

3. Request the public listing

When the checker reports the node provisionable, choose Request Listing on GitHub. Review the prefilled public node details and submit the listing request. This is a reviewed request, not automatic registration: passing eligibility does not put the node on the map by itself.

After the listing is accepted, find your node on the Network Map. Check its detail page and copied provider connection values. Only share public node metadata in the request—never wallet credentials or private configuration.

4. Submit your creator content to Fan separately

  1. In Core, open Configuration → Join Fan Network. Check the detected public origin and discoverable endpoint.
  2. Choose Test fan network readiness. Check that the endpoint is reachable and returns valid JSON. An empty result can pass the endpoint test while exposing no works; publish and make the intended work discoverable first.
  3. Choose Submit to Fan Network. Core opens a prefilled GitHub issue; review and submit it. Opening the issue does not complete admission.
  4. Once accepted and discovery has updated, check your intended public content in Certifyd Fan.

Mark this section done when: You have completed the listing and discovery steps you intend to use and checked their actual result. Network Map listing, Fan discovery, public sharing and payment readiness are separate states.

16 · PROVIDER OPERATIONS

Use Provider Console

Provider Console is the operator’s view of delegated commerce on this node. It appears when Core has the required Sovereign Node capabilities. If you cannot see it, check participation and local-service readiness first.

1. Select the creator you are reviewing

Open Provider Console from Core’s advanced menu. Start with All Active Creators, or select a specific creator under Creator Scope. Review linked creators, published items, payment intents and receipts in that scope. A new node may have no delegated activity yet.

2. Separate sales from settled payouts

Read the selected time range and whether the view uses Sale (buyer payment) or Paid (remitted execution). Review fees, payable amounts and payout states separately. A settled buyer invoice is not evidence that every collaborator has received their share; a payable amount is not a completed payout.

3. Check failures before relying on the service

Choose Refresh for current console data. Review pending, blocked or failed payout states and any runtime error shown. Return to Node → Lightning for connection, channel and liquidity checks; use Diagnostics for public connectivity problems.

It worked when: You can inspect the correct creator scope and distinguish invoice receipts, amounts owed and executed payouts. An empty console is not evidence of a fault or of successful commerce.

16 · NODE SETTINGS

What is Configuration?

It worked when: Connection, Public address, and Sharing show the active result. For extra checks, open Advanced / Diagnostics.

Optional API connections: Spotify and Cloudflare Analytics — step-by-step setup

Cloudflare Analytics is optional. It adds country, traffic, device, browser, operating-system and (where available) referral reports to Audience. It is separate from Config → Tunnel & Routing: connecting or disconnecting analytics does not change your website, DNS, tunnel, or payments. Native Audience works without Cloudflare.

Before you start: You need a Cloudflare account with access to the zone for your published creator hostname (for example, zone example.com, hostname creator.example.com). A temporary trycloudflare.com address does not provide a creator-owned Cloudflare zone to connect.

1. Create your read-only Analytics API token

  1. Sign in to Cloudflare. Open your profile's API Tokens page. Choose Create Token, then Create Custom Token.
  2. Name it Certifyd Audience Analytics.
  3. Under Permissions, add Zone → Analytics → Read. For optional Web Analytics referrers, also add Account → Account Analytics → Read.
  4. Under Zone Resources, choose Include and your specific website zone, not All zones. Under Account Resources, select the relevant account where available rather than All accounts.
  5. Continue to the summary, check the permissions, and create the token. Copy its value now and store it securely. Cloudflare displays a new token's secret only once; you cannot simply reveal an old token later. If you lost it, create a replacement or regenerate it. Do not reuse your tunnel connector token.

Use read-only analytics permissions only. Do not grant DNS, Tunnel, or write access. Never paste the token into a public page, email, screenshot, or the guide.

2. Find your zone, account, and creator hostname

  1. In Cloudflare, open the website zone (for example, example.com) and find Zone ID on the zone's Overview page. Copy it.
  2. Find Account ID in the selected account's overview/details. This field is optional in Core and is used for Web Analytics referral reporting when supported.
  3. Use the exact public hostname where fans reach your creator page (for example, creator.example.com). The zone is example.com; the hostname is creator.example.com. A hostname outside your selected zone will fail verification.

3. Connect in Certifyd Core

  1. Open Core → Config → API Connections → Cloudflare Analytics, beside the optional Spotify connection. Do not use Tunnel & Routing for this step.
  2. Enter the Zone ID and exact Creator hostname. Add Account ID if you want supported Web Analytics referral data.
  3. Paste your private Analytics Read token into the secure token field, then select Connect.
  4. Look for a verified or connected status. Select Refresh aggregates to fetch available reports, then open Audience → Traffic & Geography.

What you will see: a country map and rankings, HTTP traffic trends, devices, browsers and operating systems; referring websites appear only if Web Analytics data is accessible. Core's Buy-page views and purchases stay separate from Cloudflare HTTP requests. Sampled or incomplete Cloudflare reports may show coverage warnings; a gray country means unknown data, not necessarily zero activity.

4. Save your audience history

In Audience, choose a date range and select Export all Audience data with CSV or JSON to save available native analytics and locally cached Cloudflare aggregates. Export current page covers that page's native metrics only. These files are reports, not a restorable Core database backup; use Core's backup feature for recovery.

Having trouble connecting?

  • Unauthorized / missing permission: Verify Zone → Analytics → Read and that Zone Resources includes the selected zone. Web Analytics may also require Account → Account Analytics → Read.
  • Hostname unverified: Make sure the hostname belongs to the selected Cloudflare zone; don't enter an unrelated site.
  • Token lost or expired: Create a replacement or regenerate it in Cloudflare, then connect again. Previously created token secrets cannot be viewed again.
  • No geography yet: Refresh aggregates, check the hostname and reporting period, and allow for Cloudflare sampling, retention, and availability limits.
  • No referrers: Add the correct Account ID, check Web Analytics access for the hostname, and try a shorter reporting period. Not all zones or ranges return this dataset.
  • Cloudflare unavailable: Try again later. Native Audience analytics and commerce continue independently.

Cloudflare's official token guide has the latest dashboard wording. Your analytics token stays in Core and is never needed to operate a tunnel.

Technical addresses

The private dashboard/API uses 127.0.0.1:4000. The public creator pages use 127.0.0.1:4010. Never point your public domain at the private dashboard.

18 · COLLABORATION

Edit a split (if sharing)

A split says who shares in a work and what percentage each person gets. Basic creators cannot create or accept a shared split. Both you and the people you invite need the required permanent identity and setup. Core checks this for each person. Start with your draft in Works; Manage Splits lists published works.

  1. In Works → Your Works → Certifyd Works, find the draft and press Edit splits.
  2. Press Add participant for each person. Enter their creator ID or email, their role, and their percentage. Include your own share. Intended total must be 100%.
  3. Press Save. Core creates invites. Open Split Invites, copy each link or token, and send it to the right person yourself.
  4. Wait for everyone to accept. Check that Active total is 100% with no required invite pending, then press Lock. Core saves a tamper-evident record of the agreed split.
  5. Finish the work details and publish it. The accepted participants will see the published work in their Library and can share it to their profiles. To change a locked split later, create a new version.

If Edit splits is unavailable, read the reason Core shows. Show Advanced does not remove these requirements.

COMMERCE · BASIC

Why are some Commerce tools unavailable?

In Basic, you can create a profile, prove an identity, make a solo work, share a temporary link, and see Buy-page views in Audience. Paid-purchase reporting and the Revenue, Royalties, Rights Holders, and Manage Splits tools need Sovereign setup.

For a permanent address and payments, choose Sovereign Creator above. You will need a domain and a payment provider, and Core will check the rest. Choose Sovereign Node if you run Bitcoin and Lightning services for other creators.

20 · ACTIVITY

Read the Audience tab

See how people reach your work. Core records Buy-page views for Basic and Sovereign creators; paid purchases and an indicative view-to-purchase ratio appear when commerce is available.

What to click: Open Audience. Choose Last 7 days, Last 30 days, Last 90 days, or All time. Use Refresh for the latest report. Select Export all Audience data and CSV or JSON when you want one file with every owned work and available history.

It worked when: You see your recorded views and a per-work list, or an accurate zero if no views occurred during a covered period. A coverage warning means Core cannot confirm all earlier activity. Cloudflare traffic is separate from Core views and never used to calculate conversion.

Want a country map and traffic reports? Follow Configuration → Optional API connections → Cloudflare Analytics. Cloudflare requests are separate from Core Buy-page views; missing countries mean unknown data, not zero traffic.

21 · MONEY VIEWS

Understand Revenue

Revenue shows several stages of getting paid. Overview shows status, Sales shows purchases, Content Intelligence shows each work’s performance, Earnings shows your share, and Payouts shows payments sent to you.

A sale does not mean you have received a payout. Core may keep this tab locked until your payment setup is ready.

22 · PARTICIPATION

Understand Royalties

Royalties shows works you are part of: your own work, accepted shared splits, and other connected works. Use it to see your role in a work.

Being listed on a work does not mean you have been paid. Check Revenue for sales, earnings, and payouts.
