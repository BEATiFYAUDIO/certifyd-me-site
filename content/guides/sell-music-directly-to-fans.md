Selling music directly starts with a simple test: can somebody find your work, understand the offer, pay for it and receive the access you promised?

A buy button is only one part of that journey. You also need accurate release information, a dependable delivery path and a way to help the buyer if something goes wrong. Use this guide to plan the whole sale before sending fans to it.

## 1. Prepare the work and the offer

Choose the exact release file and artwork. State what the buyer receives: a download, supported playback, access to a particular work, or a clearly described bundle. Explain the format and any limitations. Do not promise lifetime access unless you can support that commitment.

Confirm that you have the necessary permissions to sell the recording and underlying material. Keep contributor and rights records together using the [music metadata checklist](/guides/music-metadata-checklist/). A successful upload does not establish permission to sell.

## 2. Publish a page somebody else can understand

Give the work a clear title, artist credit, useful description and recognizable artwork. Keep the public page distinct from your private management screen. Check the page while signed out: it should describe the offer without exposing private files or account settings.

For a concrete example, the [Can It Run Core? demonstration](/blog/can-it-run-core-your-computer-your-network/) shows Core management, an existing published work, public discovery and a recorded payment/unlock. It demonstrates that captured beta workflow; it is not evidence that every deployment or configuration behaves identically.

## 3. Make the published work discoverable

A public link and discovery are separate steps. Check where the work appears: [your public profile](/profile/), your own site, a discovery service or a link you share with fans. Confirm that those links open the intended work.

In the recorded Certifyd workflow, creators manage work in Core and fans discover public work through [Certifyd Discovery](https://fan.certifyd.me/). Availability depends on the installation and public-service setup. A dashboard running locally does not by itself establish a reachable storefront.

The [EVEN analysis](/blog/even-josh-remsberg-direct-to-fan-operating-layer/) and [Fanfare coverage](/blog/fanfare-independent-music-charts-sales-not-streams/) explore different ways direct fan activity can matter beyond streaming totals.

## 4. Set the price and explain checkout

Make the price, currency and included access visible before checkout. Check fees, payment-method requirements, refunds and support responsibilities for the system you choose. Decide how you will handle a buyer who pays but cannot retrieve the work.

Certifyd's published demonstration includes a genuine Bitcoin Lightning payment and content unlock. Do not generalize that into universal payment-method support, a guaranteed settlement time or a particular payout split. A public work link, a paid invoice, buyer access and creator settlement are different states.

Our [SoundCloud paid-download analysis](/blog/soundcloud-paid-downloads-artists-keep-100-one-platform-problem/) and [creator payments/direct-commerce coverage](/blog/digiday-creator-economy-payments-direct-commerce/) explain why fees and the checkout boundary deserve attention.

## 5. Confirm payment and access separately

Test the buyer journey with an authorized test purchase in your chosen system. Confirm the offer, payment result and access to the same work. Check what happens if the browser reloads or the buyer returns later; only promise recovery paths you have actually verified.

Keep the relevant order or receipt reference and tell buyers where to get support. A receipt is a record associated with a transaction; it is not copyright ownership. Do not assume a local access history is a complete accounting ledger or that purchase confirmation proves every creator payout has settled.

| Stage | What to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Offer | Correct work, amount, currency and access terms |
| Checkout | Supported method and clear pending/failed states |
| Payment | Genuine confirmation rather than an invoice merely being displayed |
| Access | The purchased work really opens or downloads as promised |
| Creator settlement | The payment records support any claim about funds received |
| Return visit | The advertised receipt/access recovery path works |

## 6. Keep the customer relationship useful and consensual

Give fans a stable place to find you and a clear support route. If you offer email updates, ask for consent and explain how to leave. A purchase does not automatically mean somebody has joined a mailing list.

Check which customer records you can export, what you are allowed to retain and how a fan can contact you if you change services. Our [Patreon and platform-dependency analysis](/blog/patreon-says-internet-failed-creators-is-another-platform-the-answer/) examines why access to an audience and control of a relationship are different questions.

## 7. Choose the operating work you can sustain

A hosted service may handle checkout, hosting and support tools for you, with its own policies and fees. Running creator infrastructure puts more operational responsibility on you: updates, backups, connectivity, public availability and payment configuration. Verify the actual responsibilities before choosing.

Certifyd Core is creator infrastructure that can run on your computer. Start with the [current installation guide](/core/guide/) and choose the correct release on [Downloads](/downloads/). Installation is a first step; it is separate from public publishing and payment readiness.

Your next action: write down one offer and walk through its public page, checkout, payment confirmation and access recovery as a buyer would. Fix the first unclear step before promoting it. Direct selling works best when the fan does not have to understand your infrastructure to enjoy your music.
