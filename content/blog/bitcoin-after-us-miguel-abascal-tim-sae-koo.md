# Bitcoin After Us: Two Fathers, One Mission, and a Future Worth Building

*Certifyd Sovereign Creator Spotlight: Miguel Abascal and Tim Sae Koo*

What good is building wealth if the next generation doesn't know what to do with it?

It's a question that goes well beyond Bitcoin. Parents spend their lives working, saving, building businesses and trying to create opportunities for their children. But passing down money is one thing. Passing down the knowledge, discipline and independence needed to preserve it is something else entirely.

That's the conversation behind **Bitcoin After Us**, a podcast created by two fathers and entrepreneurs, Miguel Abascal and Tim Sae Koo.

Together, they're exploring what it means to prepare the next generation for a world that looks very different from the one their parents inherited. Bitcoin is central to that discussion, but so are education, responsibility, entrepreneurship and the values families pass from one generation to another.

At Certifyd, we're proud to feature creators who have something meaningful to contribute. Miguel and Tim aren't simply talking about financial independence. They're sharing the experiences and ideas that have shaped how they think about their children's futures.

And in Miguel's case, there's another connection we're especially excited about: he's putting creator sovereignty into practice by operating his own Certifyd Core.

## Miguel Abascal: From the banking world to Bitcoin Sherpa

Miguel Abascal didn't arrive at Bitcoin through the usual route.

Originally from Mexico City, Miguel helped grow a Mexican coffee business before moving to Canada in 2010 and rebuilding his career in financial services. He went on to work with major institutions, including TD Bank and Scotiabank, gaining firsthand experience with the systems that shape how businesses and individuals manage money.

That's an interesting background for someone who would eventually become known as **Bitcoin Sherpa**.

Miguel understands traditional finance because he's worked inside it. He's seen how financial products are built, how businesses operate and how institutions manage substantial portfolios. His interest in Bitcoin grew alongside that experience, rather than in isolation from it.

His career eventually expanded into Bitcoin financial technology, including work with Fedi and BitBooks. Today, he also advises business owners on how to operate more effectively and retain more of the value they create.

That combination gives Miguel an interesting perspective. He understands why financial institutions exist and what they do well, but he's also interested in what becomes possible when individuals have greater control over their own money.

For Miguel, Bitcoin isn't simply an asset to buy and hope appreciates. It's part of a larger conversation about ownership, financial responsibility and independence.

### Advice That Worked: Learning from people who've built something

Miguel also hosts *Advice That Worked*, a podcast built around a simple idea: ask successful entrepreneurs and business leaders what advice actually made a difference.

Not the motivational slogans. Not the polished success stories. The practical lessons that helped someone make a better decision, overcome a challenge or build a business.

Those conversations give entrepreneurs an opportunity to share what they've learned through experience. They also create something valuable for listeners who may be facing similar decisions in their own businesses.

There's a common thread running through Miguel's work. Whether he's discussing Bitcoin, advising business owners or interviewing entrepreneurs, he's interested in helping people become more capable of making their own decisions.

That philosophy carries directly into Bitcoin After Us.

## Tim Sae Koo: Building wealth is only half the job

Tim Sae Koo approaches many of the same questions from the perspective of entrepreneurship, fatherhood and generational responsibility.

As the founder of **Generation Bitcoin**, Tim explores how families can build financial resilience while helping children develop a better understanding of money and the decisions that shape their futures.

His work connects financial education with the practical realities of raising a family. After all, it's one thing to accumulate assets. It's another to prepare someone else to manage them responsibly.

Parents can leave behind savings, property, businesses or Bitcoin. But what happens if their children haven't developed the knowledge or judgment needed to handle those things?

That's where Tim's interests become especially relevant.

Financial independence isn't simply a number in an account. It involves understanding risk, making decisions, developing useful skills and learning how to create value. Those lessons aren't automatically transferred alongside an inheritance.

Tim brings his experience as an entrepreneur and father into that conversation, making questions about generational wealth feel personal rather than theoretical.

Together, he and Miguel bring different professional backgrounds to a shared concern: **how do we give our children more than money?**

## Bitcoin After Us: What are we really leaving behind?

The title *Bitcoin After Us* says a lot about the podcast.

Bitcoin may continue operating long after today's holders are gone. But what happens to the wealth, knowledge and responsibility associated with it when one generation hands things over to the next?

That's the question Miguel and Tim are exploring.

Their conversations cover Bitcoin inheritance, financial literacy, parenting, education and the challenges of preparing children for an uncertain economic future. Rather than treating Bitcoin as a magic solution to every financial problem, they examine what ownership actually demands from the people who hold it.

One of their opening episodes, *The Bitcoin Inheritance Paradox and Why Proof of Work Is the Answer*, explores the difference between inheriting something valuable and understanding what makes it valuable.

It's an important distinction. Someone can inherit Bitcoin without understanding custody, financial risk or the discipline required to preserve wealth. The asset may transfer successfully while the knowledge behind it does not.

Another episode, *The Future Gap: Why I Choose Bitcoin as a New Dad*, brings those questions into the experience of becoming a parent. It examines the financial challenges facing younger generations and why Bitcoin has become part of the conversation for families thinking decades ahead.

What makes Bitcoin After Us interesting is that these aren't abstract debates about monetary policy. They're conversations between fathers thinking about their own children.

And that's something many people can relate to, whether they own Bitcoin or not.

The bigger question is what we're doing today to prepare the people who come after us.

## From financial sovereignty to creator sovereignty

There's a reason we're particularly proud to have Miguel participating in Certifyd.

Much of the Bitcoin conversation is about reducing dependence on financial intermediaries. Bitcoin allows people to hold and transfer value without requiring a bank to maintain the underlying ledger or custody their funds.

But financial independence is only one part of a person's digital life.

What about the work they create? Their identity? Their audience? Their published ideas?

A musician, writer, filmmaker or podcaster can own the copyright to their work while still depending entirely on somebody else's platform to maintain their public identity, distribute their content and connect them with an audience.

That's the problem Certifyd is working to change.

**Certifyd Core runs on the creator's own computer.** It provides a foundation for establishing creator identity, managing work and publishing content from infrastructure the creator operates.

Instead of requiring every creator to exist as an account inside one central platform, Certifyd is building a network of independently operated creator infrastructure.

For someone like Miguel, whose work already explores the relationship between ownership and independence, that connection makes a lot of sense.

## The connection between Bitcoin and Certifyd

Bitcoin and Certifyd solve different problems, but they share an important architectural idea.

In a traditional financial system, people generally depend on institutions to maintain accounts, process transactions and provide access to financial services.

Bitcoin introduced another possibility: a network where individuals can participate without requiring one central institution to operate the entire system.

Much of today's creator economy still follows the older centralized model. Creators join platforms, build audiences inside them, upload their work and depend on those companies to maintain access and commercial relationships.

Certifyd approaches creator infrastructure differently.

Creators can operate Core themselves. Independent nodes can connect. Applications can provide discovery and fan experiences without needing to become the permanent home of every creator's identity and work.

Bitcoin and Lightning also provide payment options within Certifyd, supporting direct creator commerce where those capabilities are enabled.

This doesn't mean every creator needs to become a technical expert, just as using Bitcoin doesn't mean everyone needs to run every part of the network.

It means the underlying architecture can give individuals a more direct role in the systems they depend on.

For Miguel, Bitcoin Sherpa and Bitcoin After Us, that relationship between ownership, independence and responsibility is especially fitting.
