# Music Ally’s Startups 2026 Report Shows Music’s Infrastructure Questions Are Still Open

Music Ally’s **Startups 2026** insight report arrives with a useful reminder: music’s hardest problems are not confined to one product category. Its SI:X finalists work across sync licensing, fan-data aggregation, curation-driven streaming, payment technology and virtual instruments. For creators, partners and investors, that range matters because each category touches a different part of the work required to turn music into a sustainable business.

Music Ally describes SI:X as its showcase, contest and mentorship programme for early-stage startups in and around music. The 2026 cycle is the fifth edition since SI:X launched in 2022, with Amazon Music, Ascap Lab, ATC Music Group, Reed Smith, Shot Tower Capital and Universal Music Group Digital Innovation named as programme partners.

## A crowded map of unfinished work

The immediate takeaway is healthy startup activity across licensing, audience information, listening experiences, payments and music-making tools. But a broad field of products is not automatically a new industry structure. The report preview does not establish that these companies share a common rights model, data model or route to stronger creator control.

That distinction is important. A sync product can improve a licensing workflow; a fan-data service can make audience information easier to interpret; payment technology can streamline a transaction. Yet creators can still face a business spread across separate services, each holding part of the context needed to identify a work, understand who is connected to it and manage permission to use it.

## The question beneath the product categories

Rights authorization, provenance and attribution are often treated as back-office details. They are the links between a work, the people attached to it, the permission to use it and the activity built around it.

When those links are held in disconnected company systems, a creator can gain a useful new service while remaining dependent on the rules, accounts and records of intermediaries they do not operate. That is the gap beneath the startup map: specialized products may solve individual tasks, but creators still need an operating foundation that is not owned by whichever service sits between them and their work.

## Moving beyond the account model

This is where the distinction between a tool and infrastructure matters. A creator account gives access to a company’s environment; creator-operated infrastructure changes who can participate in operating the environment itself. The question is not whether every music function should be rebuilt at once. It is why core creator context must always begin inside a centralized company’s system.

Certifyd’s public network currently includes a Join Network path, a Register Node page and public creator-facing links and profiles. Those are present, public signs of a network direction rather than another closed creator account. Certifyd’s broader creator-owned infrastructure thesis remains in beta: a shared foundation intended to support multiple creator-business products.

That architecture does not claim to replace the licensing, data, streaming or payment businesses represented in Music Ally’s cohort today. It sets a different destination: creators participating in the infrastructure around their work, rather than repeatedly placing their operating context under the control of each new intermediary. If that direction develops, a creator’s role can become more durable than access to an account or dashboard.

## A useful measure for the next wave

Music Ally’s 2026 finalists show that founders are still finding real problems worth solving. The next question for the sector is more demanding: do new products merely improve individual steps, or do they help move the underlying control of creator operations closer to creators themselves?

That question applies equally to licensing, fan data, payments, streaming and creative tools. The strongest infrastructure will not only make music-business tasks easier. It will give creators a meaningful role in operating the systems that carry the context behind those tasks.
