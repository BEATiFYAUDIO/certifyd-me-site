# Voice-Swap Joins DDEX as AI Voice Rights Move Into the Music Supply Chain

Voice-Swap has joined DDEX as a full member, with the company saying it wants to help shape interoperable metadata for AI-powered voice models. According to the announcement, its proposed areas of contribution include voice-model identity, consent and authorisation metadata, usage-scoped permissions, provenance and rights reporting.

The immediate story is about standards. The larger story is about where permission lives once an AI voice moves beyond the service that licensed or generated it.

## Permission has to survive the handoff

AI voice is entering production, distribution and commercial workflows. A permission recorded at the moment of licensing is not enough if a later producer, distributor or commercial partner cannot tell which model was used, who authorised it and whether the use remains within scope.

That turns consent from a deal-level detail into operational data. It needs to travel with the work in a form that other systems can read. DDEX is a natural place for that conversation because the problem crosses company boundaries.

The standards are not settled, and questions remain about what is copyrightable in the AI-voice field. But Voice-Swap’s move signals that rights information is being pushed earlier into the production chain, rather than being reconstructed after a recording has already moved downstream.

## Interoperability is not the same as control

Machine-readable permission data could make AI voice use easier to interpret across the supply chain. Yet a separate question remains: must the company that licenses, generates or manages an AI voice also remain the place where the creator’s operating records are held?

When the authorization history, work context and release information sit inside intermediary-controlled systems, creators gain access to useful services but depend on those companies’ rules, records and relationships. Interoperability can improve how data moves between systems; it does not by itself make the creator an operator of the infrastructure carrying that data.

That is the architectural issue beneath the metadata discussion. AI voice may require shared standards, but creators also need a way to keep their own creative context from existing only as an account history inside the services involved in a production or licensing chain.

## A creator-operated starting point

Certifyd is building toward creator-operated infrastructure and a network that moves supported creator functions away from a centrally owned operating environment. The point is not simply to make information portable after an intermediary has collected it. It is to give creators a starting point for operating the context around their work rather than leaving that context solely inside the systems of AI-voice services, distributors or other third parties.

Certifyd Core supports release records that preserve work, release and attribution context for creator workflows. In an AI-voice workflow, that gives the creator a place to maintain the work and release context around a recording as production and commercial uses become more complex. The practical difference is that this part of the creator operation can originate in creator-operated infrastructure, instead of being limited to records controlled by whichever service handled a particular stage.

Release records do not settle consent standards or answer every AI-voice rights question. But they establish a current foundation for the broader direction Certifyd is building toward: creators operating more of the infrastructure around their work, rather than remaining only account holders inside an intermediary’s environment.

## The next test is whether standards work outside the originating service

Voice-Swap’s DDEX participation puts the right question on the table: can AI-voice authorization become information that the wider music supply chain can interpret? The answer will depend on the standards that emerge and on whether downstream systems can use them consistently.

For creators, the lasting issue is not only whether AI voice can be licensed. It is whether the identity, authorization and release context around that use can remain intelligible without being trapped inside the company that first handled the transaction.
