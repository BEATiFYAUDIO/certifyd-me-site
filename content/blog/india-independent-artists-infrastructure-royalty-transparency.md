# India’s Independent Artists Need Infrastructure, Not Another Gatekeeper

Music Business Worldwide’s October 13 report on Madverse Music Group puts a sharp question at the center of India’s independent music market: can artists gain global access without handing more of their working lives to systems they do not control? Madverse founder and CEO Rohan Nesho Jain says independent artists need infrastructure rather than gatekeepers. He founded the company in New Delhi in 2023, in a market where film-based releases accounted for 57% of music consumption in 2025, according to the FICCI-EY India media and entertainment report cited by MBW.

## The gap is bigger than distribution

Jain’s argument is not simply that Indian artists need more exposure. He describes a choice between global platforms that were not built for India and local operators with opaque systems and insufficient technology behind them. That is a practical problem for artists trying to understand how their work travels, where money is generated and how they reach listeners beyond their home market.

Royalty transparency, local knowledge and global reach are often treated as separate benefits. They are parts of the same operating layer. An artist cannot make an informed decision about a release, a partner or a career path when the information behind those decisions sits in disconnected or opaque systems.

## Better services can still concentrate control

The immediate answer is to build more capable services for independent artists. That matters in a market shaped by film music and a rising non-film presence: Spotify reported that royalties generated by Indian artists rose 29% year over year in 2025, while non-film I-Pop took half of its year-end Top 10 in India.

Yet access delivered through an intermediary remains access governed by that intermediary. If identity, publishing context, commerce activity and public-facing records are assembled inside services run by other companies, the creator is still an account in someone else’s operating environment. The company sets the rules, controls the relationships and holds together the information an artist needs to operate.

## Infrastructure should not require a gatekeeper

India should not have to choose between locally relevant services and credible global connections. The more fundamental question is why the core information around a creator’s work has to begin inside a centralized company at all.

That is the dependency exposed by Jain’s critique. Without a creator-controlled foundation, an artist’s working identity, publishing context, commerce activity and public records remain fragmented across companies—or concentrated inside one company’s system. Better dashboards or clearer reporting can improve that experience, but they do not change who operates the underlying layer.
