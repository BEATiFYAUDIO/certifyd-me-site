## The Future of Music Chart Eligibility: Majors, Believe, BMG, Concord, HYBE Propose New Rules for AI Generated Songs

As artificial intelligence continues to evolve, so too does the landscape of music chart eligibility. Major labels like Sony Music Entertainment (Majors), Believe, Universal Music Group (BMG), Concord Music Group, and others are now considering new rules for AI-generated songs on their charts.

### The Current Landscape

Currently, many major record labels have strict guidelines that determine whether a song can be included in their chart. These rules often rely heavily on human judgment and subjective criteria, which can lead to inconsistencies and biases. For example, a song might be deemed eligible based on the artist's reputation rather than its actual merit.

### The Proposed Changes

To address these issues, several major music labels have proposed new eligibility rules for AI-generated songs. These changes aim to introduce more objective standards that are less prone to human error or bias. Here’s how their proposals could impact the industry:

#### 1. **Transparency and Provenance**

The first step in creating a fairer system is ensuring transparency about the creation process of AI-generated songs. This means clearly documenting every stage of the songwriting, production, and mastering processes to provide a clear record of who contributed what.


#### 2. **Machine Learning Algorithms**

Implementing machine learning algorithms that can independently evaluate the quality of an AI-generated song based on specific criteria such as melody, rhythm, harmony, lyrics, and overall composition. These algorithms should be trained to recognize patterns and features that are indicative of human creativity.

#### 3. **Human Oversight**

While machine learning is designed to minimize bias, it cannot replace the role of humans entirely. Therefore, a system that incorporates human oversight can help ensure that AI-generated songs meet certain standards without being overly influenced by biases or subjective judgments.
