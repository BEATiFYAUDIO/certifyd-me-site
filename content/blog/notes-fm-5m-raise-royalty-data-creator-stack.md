# Notes.fm’s $5M Raise Signals That Royalty Data Is Moving Into the Creator Stack

Notes.fm has raised $5 million from artists, managers, executives and music-industry companies, including Benny Blanco, Zach Bryan, Tainy, Ari Emanuel and Julie Greenwald. The company says the funding will support further product development and global marketing as it expands its royalty-collection offering.

Notes.fm says it helps musicians and music companies identify and collect royalties. It charges a flat monthly subscription fee rather than taking a share of distribution and publishing royalties. Its catalog review technology is positioned to help rights owners find missing royalties and address the issues behind them.

## Royalty recovery is no longer just back-office work

The obvious takeaway is investor confidence in a service built around money that creators and rights holders may fail to collect. But the larger shift is that royalty operations are moving closer to the center of an artist’s working stack.

For years, distribution, publishing administration, credits and royalty recovery could sit in separate systems and arrive at different points in a release cycle. That separation makes errors harder to spot and harder to fix. Notes.fm’s announcement, including its Credits.fm database of more than 150 million song codes and credits, reflects demand for systems that connect the release process to the data that determines who can be paid.

## The changing boundary is between release and settlement

A release is not simply a file delivered to services. It is also a set of credits, identifiers and rights details that must survive across the systems handling the work. When those details are incomplete or disconnected, recovery becomes a later investigation rather than a normal part of operating a catalog.

That changes the competition for independent-music tools. Artists and their teams are not only looking for help getting music into the market. They increasingly need a workable record of what the music is, who participated and what information follows it through distribution, publishing and royalty workflows.

## Records need to begin with the creator

Notes.fm is solving a real problem. When distribution, publishing, credits and royalty collection live in different places, mistakes are harder to find and money can go missing. Bringing more of that work together can make royalty recovery easier.

But there is still a bigger question: who holds the underlying record of the release?

If that record lives mainly inside a distributor, publisher, royalty service or other intermediary, the creator is still dependent on that company to maintain the context around the work. Change services, find conflicting information or discover a missing credit, and the artist may have to piece the history together again.

## A new expectation for artist operations

Notes.fm's $5 million raise is another sign that credits, royalty data and release information are becoming part of the everyday creator stack. They are no longer just administrative details to clean up after a song is released.

The next step is bigger than building better recovery tools. **Creators need their own durable record of the work those tools are trying to reconcile.**

A distributor can help distribute. A publisher can administer publishing. A royalty service can help find missing money. Certifyd's role is different: **give the creator a foundation they control underneath those changing relationships.**

That's why Notes.fm is interesting from a Certifyd perspective. Its growth validates the problem: fragmented music data costs creators money. The architectural question Certifyd adds is whether solving that problem should mean putting more of the creator's business into another service—or giving the creator control of the underlying record in the first place.
