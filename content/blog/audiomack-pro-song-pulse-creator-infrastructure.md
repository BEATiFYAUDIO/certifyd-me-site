# Audiomack Pro Turns Artist Tools Into a More Central Operating Layer

Audiomack has rolled out Audiomack Pro globally, putting advanced analytics, priority support, a profile verification badge, Boost Bump promotion and access to its payouts program into a paid artist subscription. The plan costs $3.99 a month or $39.99 a year. Its new Song Pulse feature shows artists when listeners engage with a song and helps them turn those moments into videos for TikTok, Instagram and YouTube.

## A dashboard becomes part of the artist operation

The immediate appeal is clear: artists can see engagement, act on it with short-form content, seek more reach and enter a payout program from one place. Audiomack says Boost Bump can promote tracks to listeners likely to enjoy them and deliver a 20% increase in reach. This is more than a streaming destination adding a few creator features. It brings a growing share of the work around a release inside Audiomack’s own operating environment.

Song Pulse makes that shift especially visible. Listening activity is no longer just a report delivered after a release; it becomes an input for the next promotional action. Verification, promotion and payout access sit in the same subscription relationship, which can simplify the workflow while making Audiomack’s rules, data and program access more central to the artist’s work.

## The deeper issue is control of the operating layer

A useful analytics tool does not answer the larger question: why should the service hosting audience activity also control the main route to interpret that activity, pursue reach and enter its payout program? As more creator functions move into a streaming service, the artist can gain convenience while becoming more dependent on an account inside infrastructure they do not operate.

The issue is not that artists should avoid audience tools. It is that the identity signals, engagement data, promotion pathway and payout-program entry point are increasingly gathered under one intermediary’s rules. Without creator-controlled infrastructure, the creator has no independent place in their workflow for the work, release and attribution context that sits behind activity across those functions.

## Release context beyond the account

That gap creates a need for infrastructure in which essential release context originates with the creator, rather than being assembled only inside a service’s account system. A streaming service can provide analytics and promotion, but those functions do not need to be the only place where a creator’s release context is held and organized.

In this story, that creates a different control relationship: an artist can keep that core release context in creator-facing infrastructure instead of leaving it solely within the service that displays audience activity, provides promotional tools and governs program access. That gives the creator a clearer basis for carrying their own release workflow as more activity is pulled into intermediary-operated environments.

## From account holder to infrastructure participant

Audiomack Pro offers artists a more connected set of tools, and Song Pulse gives engagement data an immediate promotional use. But the larger market shift is that streaming services are becoming creator operating layers, not just places where music is heard. The question for creators, partners and investors is whether those operating layers must remain owned by the intermediary.
