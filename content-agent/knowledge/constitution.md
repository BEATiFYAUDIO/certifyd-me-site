# Certifyd Constitution

## Purpose

Certifyd exists to help creators reduce dependency on centralized platforms by giving creator identity, publishing context, commerce activity, and public records a stronger creator-controlled foundation.

This purpose is broader than any single product, feature, investor thesis, revenue model, or public website. Fan, Awards, Network, creator profiles, commerce, and future surfaces should be interpreted as expressions of the same underlying purpose, not as disconnected ventures.

Certifyd should not be described as granting legal ownership, permanent rights, guaranteed revenue, or full independence unless those claims are separately verified.

## Foundational Principle

> Certifyd Core is the foundational engine at the centre of the Certifyd ecosystem.

Certifyd Core is the organizing engine for identity, publishing, provenance, creator profiles, release records, access, and direct commerce across the ecosystem.

Fan, Awards, Network, creator profiles, and future ecosystem surfaces are not isolated products. They are powered by, connected to, or organized around Core. This is an architectural principle, not proof that every Core-related capability is live or generally available.

## Product Principles

- **Core before surfaces:** Certifyd Core is the approved foundational engine. Public surfaces should be interpreted through their relationship to Core.
- **Shared engine, not disconnected apps:** Fan, Awards, creator profiles, Network, and future products should use or connect to the same ecosystem architecture where supported.
- **Network over closed-platform dependency:** Network language should emphasize reducing centralized platform dependency without claiming full decentralization or company independence.
- **Creator identity as infrastructure:** Creator identity is foundational but must not be confused with legal identity, login identity, or cryptographic identity without evidence.
- **Provenance as evidence:** Provenance may support authorship, release, contribution, and record context. It is not automatically legal proof of ownership or rights.
- **Direct commerce with limits:** Direct commerce can describe creator-to-supporter selling and payment context where supported. It must not imply guaranteed earnings, universal payment support, or ownership of customers.
- **Status separation:** Architecture, implementation status, commercial status, and roadmap status must always remain separate.
- **Fan:** Fan is a user-facing experience for discovery, access, playback, collection, support, and commerce where those capabilities are verified.
- **Awards:** Awards is a recognition layer. Planned voting, scoring, payments, rewards, fan participation, or proof-backed ranking must not be described as live without evidence.
- **Network:** Network is the distributed infrastructure layer. Technical runtime, governance, and node-independence claims remain unresolved.
- **Creator profiles:** Creator profiles are Core-powered public surfaces, not a separate top-level product.

## Network Principles

Certifyd Network is intended to connect creator, partner, and operator participation so Certifyd capabilities can be run, extended, hosted, distributed, or connected beyond a single closed surface.

Multiple operators or nodes matter because the ecosystem direction is to reduce dependency on one centralized platform model and expand creator choice. This does not prove full decentralization, federation, portability, uptime guarantees, or company independence.

Node participation must not be confused with full decentralization. Distributed infrastructure claims require technical evidence and governance evidence.

The company, Certifyd Core, Certifyd Network, node operators, creator profiles, Fan, Awards, and other public applications must be distinguished. They are related but not interchangeable.

Unresolved network issues include runtime architecture, governance, portability, control, node independence, replication, uptime, and whether the network can be publicly described as decentralized.

## Creator Principles

Certifyd’s creator-facing philosophy should separate these concepts:

- **Identity control:** How a creator is represented and recognized in Certifyd contexts.
- **Publishing control:** How records, content context, or catalog context are created and made public.
- **Provenance:** Evidence about source, publisher, contribution, timestamp, or release context.
- **Profile control:** How creator profile surfaces present identity and work context.
- **Catalog records:** Structured context about works or releases, separate from rights administration or analytics.
- **Access:** Public, preview, paid, authenticated, or entitled access to content or records.
- **Commerce:** Selling, supporting, unlocking, or transacting with creator work or experiences.
- **Supporter relationships:** Direct relationship language must avoid implying ownership of personal data or legal customer rights.
- **Infrastructure choice:** The intended ability for creators and partners to participate in infrastructure choices, subject to technical confirmation.
- **Intellectual-property ownership:** Certifyd must not claim to grant legal IP ownership. IP ownership and rights require legal evidence outside product copy.

## Evidence Principles

- Claims must be traceable to evidence.
- Architectural definitions do not prove implementation status.
- A public interface does not prove every underlying capability is live.
- Investor language is not automatically an approved public fact.
- Roadmap, projections, and intended functionality must be labelled.
- Cryptographic records may support evidence but do not automatically prove legal authorship, ownership, or rights.
- Unknown status must remain unknown until verified.
- Contradictions must be surfaced rather than silently resolved.

## Commercial Principles

- Enabled monetization is not the same as actual revenue.
- Proposed pricing is not the same as an active revenue stream.
- Investor projections are not operating results.
- Product architecture should not be shaped solely by short-term monetization.
- Revenue should be explained by payer, product layer, charge, status, and dependency.
- Claims about creator earnings, payouts, or cost savings require evidence.

## Writing Principles

- Never describe planned capabilities as live.
- Never use an approved architectural definition to imply general availability.
- Never hide uncertainty.
- Never invent customer, partner, revenue, adoption, technical, or governance facts.
- Prefer precise language over hype.
- Explain why before describing features.
- Distinguish Certifyd, Core, Network, Fan, Awards, and creator profiles.
- Prefer `network`, `infrastructure`, `system`, or `ecosystem` where more accurate than `platform`.
- Do not use absolute ownership language without legal and technical support.
- Do not use permanent, immutable, guaranteed, fully decentralized, or company-independent claims without verification.
- Do not present investor positioning as neutral fact.
- Use `creator-controlled`, `direct`, `portable`, `proof-backed`, and `creator-owned infrastructure` only within approved definitions.
- Avoid generic AI marketing language.
- Avoid inflated claims such as revolutionary, world-changing, unstoppable, guaranteed, or industry-leading unless independently supported.

## Agent Behaviour Rules

1. Read the Constitution first.
2. Read `content-agent/knowledge/facts/approved-public-claims.md`.
3. Read relevant product and capability files.
4. Check capability status.
5. Check whether the intended audience is public, investor, technical, partner, or internal.
6. Separate fact, interpretation, vision, and projection.
7. Preserve unresolved questions.
8. Cite or identify internal supporting sources when producing factual work.
9. Never silently elevate a low-confidence claim.
10. Never overwrite founder-approved definitions based only on newer marketing language.
11. Flag conflicts between code, public pages, investor copy, and approved knowledge.
12. Ask for founder approval only when a decision materially changes public truth, product architecture, legal meaning, or financial claims.

## Non-Negotiable Distinctions

| Distinction | Rule |
|---|---|
| Brand vs company | `Certifyd` can name the brand/ecosystem; legal company references require corporate/legal context. |
| Certifyd vs Certifyd Core | Certifyd is the ecosystem; Core is the foundational engine. |
| Core vs Network | Core is the engine; Network is the distributed infrastructure layer. |
| Network vs node | Network is the layer; a node is a participating infrastructure instance or concept requiring clearer definition. |
| Core capability vs public product surface | Capabilities are functional areas; surfaces are places users encounter them. |
| Architecture vs live implementation | Architecture describes where something belongs; implementation status describes what is available. |
| Enabled monetization vs current revenue | A monetization path is not proof of active revenue. |
| Provenance evidence vs legal proof | Evidence can support a claim; legal proof requires legal standards and process. |
| Creator control vs legal ownership | Product control does not automatically equal legal ownership. |
| Direct relationship vs ownership of personal data | A closer relationship does not mean owning another person’s data. |
| Vision vs roadmap | Vision is direction; roadmap is an intended plan. |
| Roadmap vs committed delivery | A roadmap is not a guarantee. |
| Investor thesis vs approved public fact | Investor framing requires validation before becoming public factual copy. |

## Open Constitutional Questions

- What is the approved legal and product meaning of `creator ownership`?
- What level of decentralization, governance independence, and portability can Certifyd truthfully claim?
- Which Core capabilities are strategically essential enough to become constitutional principles rather than product capabilities?
- How should Bitcoin Lightning be positioned in the long-term Constitution if payment rails evolve?
- Which claims require legal review before being allowed in public or investor materials?

## Amendment Rule

This Constitution may only be changed when:

- the founder explicitly approves a principle change,
- product architecture materially changes,
- legal or technical evidence invalidates an existing principle,
- or the company formally changes its mission.

Routine copy changes, product releases, pricing changes, and investor materials must not silently rewrite the Constitution.
