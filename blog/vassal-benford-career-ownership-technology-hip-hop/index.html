A Career Built for More Than One Generation

The modern music economy rewards speed, collaboration and cultural range. For producer and executive Vassal Benford, those demands are an extension of a career built across songs, film, business and multiple generations of artists.

Benford came of age when producers were expected to understand songs from the inside out. Harmony, arrangement, live musicianship and vocal identity were not optional details. They were the product.

Today, that same discipline operates in a very different environment—one driven by playlists, short-form video, global features, technology and increasingly genre-fluid audiences.

That range helps explain why Benford can move between artists and projects that might once have occupied completely different corners of the entertainment business.

A Jennifer Lopez project can sit beside an electronic anthem. A Jason Derulo album can connect Michael Bublé with YoungBoy Never Broke Again. A Chris Brown writing credit can coexist with film development, technology and legacy management.

Benford doesn't treat those moves as departures from one another. They are extensions of the same creative identity.

From Contemporary Music to the B.B. King Legacy

Benford's position with the B.B. King Estate makes that cross-generational strategy especially clear.

His work now reaches beyond producing new music into preserving and extending one of the most important legacies in American music.

The nearly $1 billion I-55 bridge project honoring B.B. King, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and Elvis Presley is an example of how musical legacy can move beyond recordings and become part of permanent cultural infrastructure.

For Benford, that creates an unusual bridge between two worlds: preserving the value created by previous generations while building systems for the creators working now.

And increasingly, that second part involves technology.

His position with the B.B. King Estate makes the strategy even clearer. Billboard and Variety have identified Benford as a leading executive behind projects designed to keep King’s work active across music, film and brand culture.[10] In October 2025, the Tennessee Department of Transportation listed Benford among the principals at the official Kings’ Crossing naming event. Ownership, Technology and the Hip-Hop Now

Benford's modern relevance extends beyond records and film.

Through Certifyd, the creator-focused technology company he leads, that experience is being applied to a problem he has watched develop throughout the modern entertainment economy: creators produce the underlying value, while their identity, attribution, audiences, permissions and revenue become fragmented across systems they do not control.

Certifyd is designed around a different idea.

Creators should be able to establish authorship, manage publishing and rights information, distribute their work, maintain direct relationships with audiences, and participate in commerce while retaining control over their identity and intellectual property.

The technology brings together provenance, rights management, analytics and Bitcoin Lightning payments as infrastructure creators can use rather than another platform they must surrender themselves to.

Certifyd turns Benford's philosophy into infrastructure: protect authorship and keep creators closer to the value they create.

Building Infrastructure Around the Creator

That distinction is important.

The objective isn't simply to create another destination where artists upload content. It is to give creators infrastructure they can use around their existing businesses, relationships and audiences.

For someone whose career has crossed songwriting, production, executive leadership, film, legacy management and technology, that approach is a natural continuation rather than a pivot.

The tools have changed. Distribution has changed. The speed of the industry has changed.

But the underlying principle hasn't:

Creators make the value. The infrastructure surrounding them should help them own it.
