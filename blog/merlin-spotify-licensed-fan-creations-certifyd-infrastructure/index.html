The Industry Is Moving Beyond Original Releases

Merlin's new licensing agreement with Spotify for AI-powered fan-made covers and remixes is another sign that the music industry is evolving.

For decades, derivative works lived in a legal grey area. Fans created remixes, edits, mashups, covers and alternate versions, but licensing them at scale was difficult.

Today, AI has accelerated that trend dramatically.

Millions of creators now have the tools to transform existing works into something new.

The challenge is no longer creating derivatives.

It's managing them fairly.

A Licensed Derivative Economy

Spotify's partnership recognizes something important:

Derivative works have value.

Rather than treating fan creativity as infringement, licensed ecosystems allow rights holders to authorize participation while creators produce new experiences for audiences.

Everyone benefits when permissions, attribution and compensation are handled correctly.

But licensing agreements between large companies only solve part of the problem.

The infrastructure still has to exist underneath.

Where Certifyd Takes the Next Step

Certifyd was designed with derivative creation as part of the publishing workflow—not as an afterthought.

Creators can publish original works.

Other creators can build derivative works from those originals.

Every derivative maintains a cryptographic relationship back to its source, creating a verifiable chain of attribution.

Instead of losing the connection between an original work and the creations that follow, Certifyd preserves that relationship throughout the entire lifecycle.

Clearance Before Distribution

One of the biggest challenges with remixes and AI-assisted content is obtaining permission.

Certifyd introduces a structured clearance workflow.

When someone creates a derivative work, the platform can automatically identify every upstream contributor whose approval is required.

Instead of email chains, spreadsheets and manual negotiations, clearance requests are routed directly to the appropriate rights holders.

Every approval, rejection or modification becomes part of the permanent publishing record.

Creators always know the status of a release.

Rights holders always know what they're approving.

Community and Governance

Not every project requires the same approval model.

Some creators want complete control.

Others want their communities to participate.

Certifyd supports configurable voting and clearance workflows that allow collaborators, publishers, labels or rights holders to collectively approve derivative works before publication.

This creates transparent governance around creative collaboration rather than relying on fragmented conversations across multiple platforms.

Permissions become part of the creative process.

Royalties That Follow the Work

Perhaps the biggest challenge with derivative content is ensuring the right people are paid.

Because every derivative maintains its relationship to upstream works, royalty participation doesn't have to be rebuilt every time a remix, edit or adaptation is published.

Revenue can follow the attribution chain.

Original creators remain connected to downstream works.

Derivative creators receive recognition for their contributions.

Everyone can see how a work evolved.

AI Doesn't Replace Creators

AI is becoming another creative tool.

Like a synthesizer, sampler or DAW before it, the technology itself isn't the issue.

Ownership is.

Creators need systems that can distinguish between original works, licensed derivatives, collaborative projects and unauthorized copies.

That requires verifiable provenance—not just AI detection.

What This Means for the Industry

Merlin's agreement with Spotify is another signal that licensed derivative creation is becoming part of the mainstream music business.

The next generation of platforms won't simply host AI-generated music.

They'll manage the relationships between original creators, collaborators, derivative creators, publishers, labels, fans and rights holders.

That's the infrastructure Certifyd is building.

Not just a place to upload music.

A publishing system where identity, attribution, permissions, clearance, derivative relationships and royalty participation remain connected from the first release through every remix, cover, AI adaptation and future collaboration.

Because the future of music isn't just about creating more content.

It's about proving where it came from, who contributed, who approved it, and making sure everyone gets paid.
