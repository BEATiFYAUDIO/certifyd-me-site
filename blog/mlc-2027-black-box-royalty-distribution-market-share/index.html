The MLC’s 2027 Black-Box Distribution Turns Unmatched Royalties Into a Market-Share Question

The MLC plans to begin distributing remaining 2021 black-box mechanical royalties in April 2027. The move shows why unresolved work and share information can shape not just payment timing, but who ultimately receives the money.

By CertifydPublished October 9, 2026Updated October 9, 2026
MLCmechanical royaltiesblack box royaltiesmusic publishingsongwritersrights dataCertifyd

The Mechanical Licensing Collective (MLC) plans to begin distributing remaining unmatched and unclaimed mechanical royalties from January 2021 usage in April 2027. The first-month pool is reported at $6.3 million: $3.24 million in unmatched royalties and $3.06 million in unclaimed royalties. The MLC is also tentatively preparing to distribute $75.13 million in 2021 black-box royalties on a market-share basis.

That is more than a delayed-payment story. It is a moment when money tied to particular songs and shares can move, after further matching efforts, into a pro-rata allocation based on rightsholders’ relative market shares.

From a missing match to a different allocation

The MLC says it will undertake additional matching work focused on the remaining 2021 royalties before distribution. That work matters because an unmatched or unclaimed royalty is not simply waiting in a queue forever. Once it reaches the distribution process, the remaining balance is allocated across members according to relative market share for each DSP and offering.

For songwriters, publishers, and other mechanical-rights stakeholders, the practical issue is clear: incomplete work, share, or claim information can have an economic consequence beyond a late payment. The original royalty can cease to be treated as a work-specific amount awaiting its intended recipient and become part of a pooled market-share distribution.

The rights system is making a control decision

The obvious reading is that large publishers could receive meaningful payments from a large pool. The deeper issue is architectural: a centralized rights administrator must decide what happens when its records cannot connect money to a recipient in time.

That decision is built into the system’s operating rules. The MLC has reported more than $4.2 billion in total royalties distributed to date, and it has matched and distributed substantial portions of historical black-box royalties. But the April 2027 timetable shows the boundary of a centralized matching process. When the link between usage, work, share, and recipient remains unresolved, the system has a formal path for reallocating the residual amount.

The question for creators is not only whether a collective can collect and distribute royalties. It is whether the underlying context for their works is strong enough, early enough, and durable enough to avoid becoming dependent on a later reconciliation process run elsewhere.

Creator context cannot begin only inside the intermediary

The MLC’s process concentrates matching and final redistribution decisions inside a centralized intermediary. That is necessary to its role in administering mechanical royalties, but it also exposes a gap: creators can enter the process with crucial context about a work and release already scattered across outside records. If that context is incomplete or disconnected when royalty data reaches the administrator, the creator is left dependent on a later matching process and its timetable.

Certifyd addresses that dependency at an earlier point in the chain. Its purpose is to give creator identity, publishing context, commerce activity, and public records a stronger creator-controlled foundation. Certifyd Core, currently in beta, maintains catalog context for works and releases.

That means the context around a catalog does not have to originate only in an intermediary’s system. Certifyd is building toward a network in which supported creator functions move toward creator-operated infrastructure rather than remaining entirely inside centralized operating environments. For a creator, that creates a more durable starting point for maintaining the work and release context that needs to travel through the wider rights ecosystem before a payment is difficult to place.

April 2027 is a deadline with a larger lesson

The MLC’s planned distribution still leaves room for additional matching of the 2021 royalties. But the schedule makes the stakes of unresolved data visible. A royalty that cannot be connected to its intended recipient does not remain a neutral accounting entry indefinitely.

For creators and rights holders, the lesson is simple: catalog context is not paperwork after the music is out. It is part of the infrastructure that helps preserve the connection between a work, its rights information, and the people meant to be paid.
