Digiday’s Creator Payments Warning: The Transaction Is Becoming the Relationship

Digiday reports that creator advertising spend is rising faster than the systems used to pay creators. The real opportunity is not another payment dashboard, but creator-controlled commerce that keeps the customer relationship close to the creator.

By CertifydPublished October 31, 2026Updated October 31, 2026
creator economycreator paymentsdirect commercefan relationshipsCertifydadvertising

Digiday’s report on the creator economy payments problem puts the contradiction in plain sight: creators are becoming more valuable to advertisers while the systems for paying them remain fragmented. The article, published October 29, 2026, says U.S. celebrity payments in advertising are projected to reach $2.06 billion that year, nearly 70% above the level of five or six years earlier, according to XR Extreme Reach data cited by Digiday.

The immediate issue is operational. Digiday describes payment work spread across spreadsheets, manual processes and consumer payment apps, while brands and agencies may use four or five systems on a single creator campaign. More campaigns, more categories and more creators mean more payment terms, approvals, tax checks and paperwork to reconcile.

A payment is not just back-office work

The obvious answer is better accounts-payable software. That may reduce delays and confusion, but it does not fully answer the structural problem. When a creator’s commercial life is divided across an agency’s systems, a brand’s systems and several payment vendors, the creator is treated as an account moving through someone else’s operating environment.

That setup gives intermediaries an expanding role in the creator relationship. They do not merely coordinate a campaign; they become the place where payment access, transaction history and often the practical record of the business relationship live. As creator advertising becomes a larger business, that dependency becomes harder to ignore.

Direct sales change the question

Direct commerce starts from a different premise. When fans purchase content directly from a creator, the transaction can be part of a relationship the creator develops rather than a distant entry in a third party’s workflow. The sale matters not only because money changes hands, but because it gives the creator a direct commercial connection to the people choosing to support the work.

Receipts and access are not glamorous topics, but they are where a customer relationship becomes real. A creator who can build business activity around direct purchases has a clearer path to understanding demand than one whose commercial activity is scattered across disconnected systems. That does not eliminate the need for agencies, brands or specialist payment services in advertising. It does challenge the assumption that every important creator-business function must remain inside an intermediary’s infrastructure.

The infrastructure question behind the payment problem

The payment gap Digiday identifies is evidence that the creator economy has outgrown a collection of one-off tools. The industry needs more than faster ways to send money after a deal is done. It needs a model in which creators have a stronger operating position in the commerce and customer relationships that sustain their work.

That is the specific gap Certifyd addresses in a different part of creator business: direct fan commerce. Certifyd is currently in beta and is positioned as creator-owned infrastructure for multiple creator-business products built from a shared foundation. Its beta commerce model supports direct purchases of content by fans, shifting a sale from an event administered solely within a brand, agency or payment vendor’s systems toward one that begins in creator-owned infrastructure.

It makes the underlying architectural contrast visible: a creator should not need a centralized company to sit between every commercial interaction and the customer connection it creates. As Certifyd builds toward a creator-operated network, direct purchases give creators a starting point for running more of their commerce closer to their own business rather than solely as accounts inside intermediary systems.

From payment bottleneck to creator control

Creator advertising will still require serious operational discipline as budgets rise. Digiday is right to focus on payment complexity as a constraint on the market’s next phase. But the longer-term question is who owns the infrastructure that turns commercial activity into an ongoing creator business.

Creators should not have to choose between getting paid and keeping their customer relationships intelligible. Direct commerce will not solve every campaign-payment problem, but it creates a practical alternative to building every transaction, receipt and point of access inside systems the creator does not operate. That is the direction Certifyd is building toward: a creator-owned foundation where commercial activity can begin closer to the creator, not only inside an intermediary’s account structure.
